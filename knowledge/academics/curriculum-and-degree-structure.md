# Curriculum and Degree Structure

> **Demo corpus document:** Credit values, course lists, and structures below are illustrative workshop data, not an official curriculum regulation.

## Degree Framework

The demonstration engineering degree is structured as an eight-semester program organized around a credit system. Each course carries a credit value roughly proportional to its weekly contact hours and expected workload. A student must accumulate a minimum number of credits across defined categories to graduate.

```text
Semester 1 – 2   Foundation (mathematics, sciences, programming basics, communication)
Semester 3 – 4   Core fundamentals (department-specific core subjects begin)
Semester 5 – 6   Advanced core + electives + mini-project
Semester 7 – 8   Electives + capstone project + internship window
```

## Credit Categories

A typical demonstration curriculum groups courses into categories:

- **Basic Sciences** — mathematics, physics, chemistry, applied sciences relevant to engineering.
- **Engineering Core** — foundational engineering subjects common across or specific to a department.
- **Program Core** — subjects that define the department's discipline.
- **Program Electives** — advanced, discipline-specific electives chosen by the student.
- **Open Electives** — courses taken outside the student's home department, encouraging interdisciplinary exposure.
- **Humanities and Management** — communication, ethics, economics, and professional-practice subjects.
- **Laboratory and Project Work** — hands-on courses, mini-projects, and the final-year capstone project.
- **Internship** — a mandatory or recommended industry or research internship, typically completed between the sixth and eighth semester.

The exact credit weight of each category and the minimum total required for graduation belong to the official curriculum document for a given admission year (curricula are periodically revised). Campus Copilot should not invent a specific total credit requirement unless it is present in the corpus.

## Illustrative Subject Progression by Department

The following lists are representative examples used to give the RAG system realistic subject-level vocabulary. They are not a complete or binding syllabus.

### Information Science and Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Programming Fundamentals, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Logic, Data Structures
- Semesters 3–4: Object-Oriented Programming, Database Management Systems, Operating Systems, Computer Networks, Design and Analysis of Algorithms
- Semesters 5–6: Machine Learning, Web Technologies, Software Engineering, Cloud Computing, Information Retrieval
- Semesters 7–8: Big Data Analytics, Deep Learning, program electives, capstone project

### Computer Science and Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Programming Fundamentals, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Design, Data Structures
- Semesters 3–4: Computer Organization, Database Systems, Operating Systems, Theory of Computation, Algorithms
- Semesters 5–6: Compiler Design, Artificial Intelligence, Distributed Systems, Computer Networks
- Semesters 7–8: Advanced electives (Systems, AI, Security, or Graphics tracks), capstone project

### Electronics and Communication Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Basic Electronics, Circuit Theory, Engineering Mathematics, Programming for Engineers
- Semesters 3–4: Analog Electronics, Digital Signal Processing, Electromagnetic Theory, Communication Systems
- Semesters 5–6: Embedded Systems, VLSI Design, Microcontrollers, Wireless Communication
- Semesters 7–8: RF Systems, IoT and Embedded AI electives, capstone project

### Electrical and Electronics Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Circuit Analysis, Electromagnetic Fields, Engineering Mathematics, Basic Electrical Machines
- Semesters 3–4: Power Systems, Control Systems, Power Electronics, Electrical Machines
- Semesters 5–6: Industrial Automation, Renewable Energy Systems, Instrumentation
- Semesters 7–8: Smart Grids, electives, capstone project

### Mechanical Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Engineering Mechanics, Thermodynamics, Engineering Graphics, Materials Science
- Semesters 3–4: Fluid Mechanics, Manufacturing Processes, Machine Design, Kinematics of Machines
- Semesters 5–6: Heat Transfer, CAD/CAM, Industrial Engineering, Automation and Robotics
- Semesters 7–8: Advanced manufacturing electives, capstone project

## Minors and Interdisciplinary Tracks

Students may be permitted to pursue an additional minor track — for example, a Minor in Artificial Intelligence, a Minor in Entrepreneurship, or a Minor in Data Science — by completing a defined set of open electives. Availability and eligibility depend on department capacity and the academic year's rules, so Campus Copilot should treat minor-track availability as something to verify rather than assume.

## Choosing Electives

A student choosing an elective should generally weigh:

1. alignment with their intended career direction;
2. prerequisite knowledge already covered;
3. project or research relevance;
4. faculty expertise and typical course delivery style;
5. workload relative to other commitments that semester.

Campus Copilot can help a student reason through this trade-off using information already in the corpus, but should not claim a specific elective is "the best one" without qualifying that this is a general recommendation, not an institutional determination.
