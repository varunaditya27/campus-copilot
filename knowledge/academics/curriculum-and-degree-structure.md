# Curriculum and Degree Structure

## Degree Framework

The undergraduate engineering degree is an eight-semester, credit-based program. Each course carries a credit value roughly proportional to its weekly contact hours and expected workload — typically one credit per hour of lecture, and one credit per two to three hours of laboratory or studio work. A student must accumulate a minimum of 160 credits across defined categories, along with satisfying attendance and minimum-grade requirements in every registered course, to be eligible for the degree.

```text
Semester 1 – 2   Foundation (mathematics, sciences, programming basics, communication)
Semester 3 – 4   Core fundamentals (department-specific core subjects begin)
Semester 5 – 6   Advanced core + electives + mini-project
Semester 7 – 8   Electives + capstone project + internship window
```

## Credit Categories

The curriculum groups courses into eight categories, each with a minimum credit requirement set by the Board of Studies for a given admission year:

| Category | Typical Credit Range | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Sciences | 18–22 | Mathematics, physics, chemistry, and applied sciences relevant to engineering |
| Engineering Core | 20–24 | Foundational engineering subjects common across or specific to a department |
| Program Core | 40–46 | Subjects that define the department's discipline |
| Program Electives | 15–18 | Advanced, discipline-specific electives chosen by the student |
| Open Electives | 9–12 | Courses taken outside the student's home department |
| Humanities and Management | 10–12 | Communication, ethics, economics, and professional-practice subjects |
| Laboratory and Project Work | 20–24 | Hands-on courses, mini-projects, and the final-year capstone project |
| Internship | 2–4 | A mandatory industry or research internship, normally completed between the sixth and eighth semester |

The precise credit weight of each category for a given admission cohort is published in that cohort's curriculum regulation, issued by the Board of Studies of the concerned department and ratified by the Academic Council. Students admitted in different years may be governed by different curriculum versions; a student unsure which version applies to them should check their admission year against the regulation notified for that batch, available from the department office.

## Subject Progression by Department

The lists below are representative of the core subject sequence in each department. They are illustrative of typical sequencing and do not replace the official scheme of instruction issued each year.

### Information Science and Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Programming Fundamentals, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Logic, Data Structures
- Semesters 3–4: Object-Oriented Programming, Database Management Systems, Operating Systems, Computer Networks, Design and Analysis of Algorithms
- Semesters 5–6: Machine Learning, Web Technologies, Software Engineering, Cloud Computing, Information Systems Design
- Semesters 7–8: Big Data Analytics, Deep Learning, program electives, capstone project

### Computer Science and Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Programming Fundamentals, Discrete Mathematics, Digital Design, Data Structures
- Semesters 3–4: Computer Organization, Database Systems, Operating Systems, Theory of Computation, Algorithms
- Semesters 5–6: Compiler Design, Artificial Intelligence, Distributed Systems, Computer Networks
- Semesters 7–8: Advanced electives (Systems, AI, Security, or Graphics tracks), capstone project

### Electronics and Communication Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Basic Electronics, Circuit Theory, Engineering Mathematics, Programming for Engineers
- Semesters 3–4: Analog Electronics, Digital Signal Processing, Electromagnetic Theory, Communication Systems
- Semesters 5–6: Embedded Systems, VLSI Design, Microcontrollers, Wireless Communication
- Semesters 7–8: RF Systems, IoT and Embedded Systems electives, capstone project

### Electrical and Electronics Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Circuit Analysis, Electromagnetic Fields, Engineering Mathematics, Basic Electrical Machines
- Semesters 3–4: Power Systems, Control Systems, Power Electronics, Electrical Machines
- Semesters 5–6: Industrial Automation, Renewable Energy Systems, Instrumentation
- Semesters 7–8: Smart Grids, electives, capstone project

### Mechanical Engineering

- Semesters 1–2: Engineering Mechanics, Thermodynamics, Engineering Graphics, Materials Science
- Semesters 3–4: Fluid Mechanics, Manufacturing Processes, Machine Design, Kinematics of Machines
- Semesters 5–6: Heat Transfer, CAD/CAM, Industrial Engineering, Automation and Robotics
- Semesters 7–8: Advanced manufacturing electives, capstone project

## Minors and Interdisciplinary Tracks

A student in good academic standing (CGPA of 7.0 or above at the time of application, with no active backlogs) may apply to pursue an additional minor by completing a defined set of five open electives, at least four of which must be taken outside the home department. Minors currently offered include a Minor in Artificial Intelligence, a Minor in Entrepreneurship, a Minor in Data Science, and a Minor in Embedded Systems. Applications are reviewed by the Curriculum Committee each odd semester; seats in a given minor track are capped at 40 per cohort and are allotted by CGPA in case of oversubscription. A minor is recorded on the final transcript but does not change the classification of the primary degree.

## Choosing Electives

A student choosing a program or open elective should weigh:

1. alignment with their intended career direction or higher-study plans;
2. prerequisite knowledge already covered in earlier semesters;
3. relevance to an ongoing or planned project;
4. the instructor and typical delivery style of the course, where known;
5. workload relative to other commitments in that semester, including project and internship obligations.

Elective pre-registration opens roughly three weeks before the start of a semester and closes one week before classes begin, through the department office. A department may cancel an elective with fewer than 15 registered students and require affected students to choose an alternative within five working days of the cancellation notice.

## Curriculum Revision

The curriculum for each department is reviewed by its Board of Studies at least once every four years, or sooner if triggered by significant changes in the discipline. Revisions are placed before the Academic Council for approval and, once approved, apply to newly admitted cohorts; students already admitted under an earlier regulation are ordinarily permitted to complete their degree under that regulation, with limited provision for opting into revised elective offerings where equivalence is certified by the department.
