# Academic Calendar and Semester Lifecycle

## Semester Lifecycle

The university follows two teaching semesters each academic year — an odd semester running from August to December, and an even semester running from January to May — separated by a winter break in late December and a longer summer break in June and July. Every semester follows the same broad sequence, even though exact dates shift slightly from one academic year to another:

```text
Semester orientation
      ↓
Course registration
      ↓
Teaching period (approx. 15 weeks)
      ↓
Continuous assessment
      ↓
Mid-semester evaluation (around week 8)
      ↓
Teaching completion
      ↓
End-semester examination (2–3 weeks)
      ↓
Evaluation and result processing (3–4 weeks)
      ↓
Break / next-semester preparation
```

The precise calendar for a given academic year — including the first day of classes, the mid-semester break, and the examination window — is approved by the Academic Council before the start of the odd semester and published by the Office of the Registrar on the university's official notice boards and student portal.

## Registration

Course registration confirms a student's academic selections for the semester and takes place over a five-day window immediately before the teaching period begins. Registration is completed online through the student portal for continuing students, and in person at the department office for newly admitted students. A student who has not registered by the close of the window is marked as "not registered" for that semester and must obtain written approval from the department office, along with a late-registration fee of ₹1,000, to be admitted to classes.

## Add / Drop and Course Changes

Students may add or drop an elective course during the first two weeks of the teaching period, referred to as the add/drop window, without academic penalty. A request submitted after this window requires written approval from both the course instructor and the department office, and is granted only for documented reasons such as a timetable clash discovered late or a medical circumstance. Dropping a core or program-core course is not ordinarily permitted outside the standard re-registration process described in the Grading, GPA, and Backlogs policy.

## Assessment Periods

Internal assessments, quizzes, laboratory evaluations, project reviews, and presentations are scheduled throughout the teaching period by individual course instructors, subject to the constraint that no student should have more than one internal test on a given day; the department office coordinates test schedules across courses to avoid such clashes. The mid-semester evaluation, a university-wide checkpoint in approximately the eighth week of teaching, gives students and advisors an early indication of standing before the end-semester examination.

## Examination Period

The end-semester examination period follows the completion of scheduled teaching and typically spans two to three weeks, depending on the number of registered courses across departments that semester. The final examination timetable, published by the Examinations Cell roughly three weeks in advance, is the authoritative source for a student's specific examination dates and venues; it supersedes any earlier draft schedule circulated during the semester.

## Results

Results are compiled by the Examinations Cell and published through the student portal, ordinarily within four weeks of the last end-semester examination. A provisional grade card is made available first, followed by an official transcript update after the window for revaluation requests has closed. Students should treat the portal-published result as authoritative for decisions about registration, probation, or graduation eligibility, rather than any informal indication from an instructor.

## Vacations and Breaks

The winter break runs for approximately two weeks from the third week of December, and the summer break runs for roughly eight weeks from early June to late July. Departments may schedule mandatory industry visits, workshops, or make-up laboratory sessions during a portion of the summer break with advance notice of at least four weeks; such sessions are communicated through the department office and are not treated as optional once notified.

## Public Holidays

The university observes national and regional public holidays as notified annually by the Office of the Registrar, along with a small number of institution-specific observances such as the annual Founder's Day. A list of observed holidays for the academic year is displayed on departmental noticeboards and the student portal at the start of each odd semester.
