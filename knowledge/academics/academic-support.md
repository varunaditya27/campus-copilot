# Academic Support and Learning Resources

> **Demo corpus document:** The services and procedures below are illustrative workshop data.

## Purpose

Academic support exists to help students recover from difficulties before they become major academic problems. Students should be encouraged to seek help early rather than waiting until examinations.

## Common Support Paths

### Course Instructor

The course instructor is usually the first point of contact for questions about lecture material, assignments, laboratory work, course-specific deadlines, and assessment expectations.

### Department Office

Department-level academic queries may include course registration issues, project coordination, timetable clarifications, and academic documentation. The appropriate department office should be consulted when a question requires access to institutional records.

### Academic Advising

An academic advisor can help students think through subject choices, workload, academic progression, project planning, and longer-term academic goals. Advising is especially useful when several decisions interact and the student needs context rather than a single factual answer.

### Peer Learning

Student-led study groups, coding communities, laboratory peer groups, and mentoring programs can complement formal teaching. Peer support should supplement, not replace, official academic guidance.

## Effective Help-Seeking

A useful request to an instructor or advisor should include:

1. the course or subject;
2. the specific problem;
3. what the student has already attempted;
4. any relevant deadline;
5. the type of help requested.

For example, "I am struggling with recursion" is less actionable than "I understand the base case but cannot trace the recursive calls in this problem; could you recommend two examples to practice?"

## Study Strategy

Campus Copilot can help convert a broad goal into smaller tasks:

```text
Goal
 ↓
Topics
 ↓
Priority
 ↓
Available time
 ↓
Study blocks
 ↓
Practice / review
 ↓
Progress check
```

The assistant should not claim that a particular study plan guarantees an academic outcome. It should instead help the student make a realistic plan and encourage adjustment based on progress.
