# Academic Support and Learning Resources

## Purpose

Academic support exists to help students recover from difficulty before it becomes a major academic problem — a missed concept, a slipping attendance record, or a course that has become unmanageable. Students are encouraged to seek help as soon as a difficulty appears rather than waiting until the weeks before an examination, when options for recovery are far more limited.

## Course Instructor

The course instructor is the first point of contact for questions about lecture material, assignments, laboratory work, course-specific deadlines, and assessment expectations. Most instructors hold two office hours per week, posted on their office door and in the course handout, during which students can drop in without an appointment; some instructors also accept scheduled meetings outside these hours on request.

## Department Office

The department office handles academic queries that require access to institutional records or coordination across courses — registration issues, project and internship coordination, timetable clarifications, transcript requests, and academic documentation such as bonafide certificates. The department office is generally staffed from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. on working days and is the appropriate first stop for any question that a single instructor cannot resolve alone.

## Academic Advising

Every student is assigned a faculty academic advisor at the time of admission, typically responsible for a batch of 15 to 20 advisees within the department. The advisor helps students think through subject and elective choices, workload planning, academic progression, project direction, and longer-term academic or career goals. Advising is especially useful when several decisions interact — for instance, choosing electives while also managing a heavy project load — and a student needs guidance grounded in their specific record rather than a general answer. Students under academic probation are required to meet their advisor at least once every two weeks, as described in the Grading, GPA, and Backlogs policy; other students are encouraged to check in at least once a semester, most commonly around the time of elective registration.

## Tutoring and Remedial Classes

Departments run remedial classes for foundational courses with historically high backlog rates, typically Engineering Mathematics, Programming Fundamentals, and Basic Electronics, held twice a week in the evenings in the Main Academic Block. A student who has failed a course once, or who scores below 40% in the first internal test, is automatically eligible to enroll and is notified by the department office. Enrollment is free and does not appear on the academic transcript.

## Peer Learning

Student-led study groups, departmental coding communities, laboratory peer groups, and the Student Mentorship Program — which pairs senior students with first- and second-year students in the same department — complement formal teaching. These groups typically meet in the Student Activity Centre or in the collaborative study areas of the Central Library. Peer support is a useful supplement to, but is not treated as a substitute for, guidance from an instructor or advisor on anything that affects a student's official record.

## Effective Help-Seeking

A request to an instructor or advisor is far more useful when it is specific. It should generally include:

1. the course or subject concerned;
2. the specific problem, stated precisely;
3. what the student has already attempted;
4. any relevant deadline;
5. the kind of help being requested.

For example, "I am struggling with recursion" gives an instructor little to work with, whereas "I understand the base case but cannot trace the recursive calls in this problem; could you recommend two examples to practice?" makes it possible to give a targeted answer in a single conversation.

## Building a Study Plan

Students preparing for an examination or working through a difficult topic are encouraged to break a broad goal into smaller, trackable steps:

```text
Goal
 ↓
Topics
 ↓
Priority
 ↓
Available time
 ↓
Study blocks
 ↓
Practice / review
 ↓
Progress check
```

An academic advisor or the department's remedial-class coordinator can help a student translate this outline into a concrete weekly schedule. No study plan guarantees a particular grade; students are encouraged to treat a plan as a starting point to be revised as they check their own progress against it, and to return to their instructor or advisor promptly if the plan isn't working.
