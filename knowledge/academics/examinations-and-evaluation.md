# Examinations and Evaluation

## Academic Evaluation Model

Every course is evaluated through a blended model combining continuous internal assessment with a final end-semester examination or, for project and internship courses, a final evaluation and demonstration. Continuous components can include quizzes, assignments, laboratory work, presentations, class participation, project reviews, and internal examinations. Unless the course handout states otherwise, internal assessment carries 40% weight and the end-semester examination carries 60% weight of the total course marks for theory courses; laboratory courses typically weight continuous laboratory performance at 60% and the end-semester practical examination at 40%.

The precise weight assigned to each component for a given course is set out in the course handout distributed by the instructor in the first week of the semester and filed with the department office. Students should treat that handout as the authoritative reference for a specific course's grading breakdown.

## Internal Assessment

Internal assessment is designed to give students feedback and a fair opportunity to demonstrate progress across the semester rather than resting on a single measurement. A typical theory course holds two internal tests of 90 minutes each, along with two to three smaller assignments or quizzes; the better-performing test is often weighted more heavily, as specified in the course handout. Internal marks are displayed to students, along with the answer scripts for review, within one week of each test, giving students an opportunity to flag scoring errors before the marks are finalized and submitted to the department office.

## End-Semester Examination

The end-semester examination is conducted according to the academic timetable published by the Examinations Cell, ordinarily three weeks before the examination period begins. Students should confirm their examination date, time, venue, and any course-specific instructions from the published timetable rather than from memory or informal sources, since room allocations can change between the draft and final timetable. A student who has not received a hall ticket by two working days before their first examination should contact the Examinations Cell immediately, since a valid hall ticket and student ID card are both required for entry to the examination hall.

Standard end-semester theory examinations run for three hours; students are permitted entry up to 30 minutes after the start of the examination but are not granted additional time, and are not permitted to leave within the first hour or the last 15 minutes of the examination.

## Laboratory Evaluation

Laboratory courses evaluate a combination of preparation, attendance, implementation, observation, record-keeping, viva-voce performance, and a final practical examination conducted by an internal examiner together with an external examiner drawn from a partner department or institution. Students are required to maintain a laboratory record, certified weekly by the concerned instructor; a record that is not up to date at the time of the final practical examination can result in a reduction of the observation component of the final mark.

## Project and Capstone Evaluation

Project-based courses, including the final-year capstone project, are evaluated through a sequence of milestone reviews rather than a single end-of-semester assessment:

```text
Topic selection and guide allotment
      ↓
Proposal / problem definition review
      ↓
Phase I (design) review
      ↓
Implementation
      ↓
Phase II (progress) review
      ↓
Final demonstration and viva-voce
      ↓
Report submission and documentation
```

Each review is conducted by a panel typically comprising the project guide and two additional faculty members from the department, and contributes a defined share of the final project grade, set out in the project course handbook issued at the start of the capstone year. A student who misses a scheduled review without prior written approval from the project guide forfeits the marks allotted to that review.

## Revaluation and Academic Queries

A student who believes an evaluation — internal or end-semester — has been recorded incorrectly should raise the matter first with the course instructor, and if unresolved, follow the revaluation process administered by the Examinations Cell, described in the Grading, GPA, and Backlogs policy. Queries about a specific mark, timetable slot, or venue should go to the Examinations Cell or the department office rather than being inferred from general policy.

## Exam-Day Checklist

Before an examination, a student should verify:

- the examination date and time from the final published timetable;
- the examination venue and seating number, if allotted;
- that they are carrying their hall ticket and student ID card;
- permitted stationery or materials for that specific paper;
- the reporting time, normally 20 minutes before the scheduled start;
- any special instructions issued for laboratory or practical examinations, including required lab coats or safety equipment.
