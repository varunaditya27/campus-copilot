# Grading, GPA, and Backlogs

## Grading Scale

The university uses a ten-point letter-grade scale for every credit-bearing course:

| Letter Grade | Grade Point | Approximate Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O | 10 | Outstanding |
| A+ | 9 | Excellent |
| A | 8 | Very Good |
| B+ | 7 | Good |
| B | 6 | Above Average |
| C | 5 | Average |
| P | 4 | Pass |
| F | 0 | Fail / Backlog |

A course result below grade point 4 is recorded as a backlog and must be cleared through a supplementary examination or re-registration, depending on the course type, before the credits are counted toward the degree.

## SGPA and CGPA

Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) and Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) are computed as credit-weighted averages:

```text
SGPA = Σ (course credits × grade point) / Σ (course credits)     — for one semester
CGPA = Σ (course credits × grade point) across all completed semesters / Σ (all credits)
```

For example, a student who takes three courses in a semester with credits 4, 3, and 3, and grade points 8, 9, and 7 respectively, has:

```text
SGPA = (4×8 + 3×9 + 3×7) / (4+3+3) = (32 + 27 + 21) / 10 = 8.0
```

A backlog course carries a grade point of zero but its credits are still included in the denominator until the backlog is cleared, after which the CGPA is recalculated using the cleared grade. The Examinations Cell publishes an updated grade card within ten working days of a supplementary result being finalized.

## Backlogs and Supplementary Examinations

A backlog — an "F" grade, or a missed examination without an approved exemption — is cleared through one of two routes:

1. A supplementary examination, offered in the examination cycle immediately following the original one, for theory courses. Registration for a supplementary examination opens two weeks after results are published and carries a fee of ₹500 per course.
2. Re-registration and re-attendance of the course, required for laboratory, project, and internship courses where the practical or continuous-assessment component cannot be reasonably reassessed through a written examination alone. Re-registration is done through the department office during the normal course-registration window and is charged at the standard per-credit fee.

A student may carry a maximum of four active backlogs at any time. A student who accumulates more than four active backlogs is barred from registering for courses in the next higher semester until the count is brought within the limit, and is required to meet with their academic advisor before re-registration is approved. Backlogs from the first four semesters must ordinarily be cleared before a student is permitted to sit the eighth-semester end examinations.

## Revaluation and Re-evaluation

These two processes are administered separately by the Examinations Cell:

- **Revaluation** is a formal recheck of an already-evaluated answer script, carried out by a different examiner, and can be requested within seven calendar days of results being published. The revaluation fee is ₹300 per course, refunded in full if the grade improves by one full letter grade or more. A revalued result is final and cannot itself be revalued.
- **Re-examination**, used mainly for backlog clearance, means reattempting the assessment itself rather than rechecking an existing script. It follows the supplementary examination process described above.

Revaluation requests are submitted through the department office using the prescribed Revaluation Request form, along with the fee receipt; results of revaluation are typically published within three weeks of the request window closing.

## Academic Probation and Standing

A student whose CGPA falls below 5.0 at the end of any semester, or who is carrying more than four active backlogs, is placed under academic probation. Probation carries mandatory fortnightly meetings with an assigned academic advisor, a recommended reduced course load for the following semester, and a formal review by the department's Academic Progress Committee at the end of the probationary semester. A student who fails to raise their CGPA above 5.0 within two consecutive probationary semesters is referred to the Dean of Academics for further review, which may include a recommendation to repeat a semester.

A student's current academic standing is recorded on their official transcript, maintained by the Examinations Cell, and should be confirmed there rather than estimated from a partial set of results.

## Grade Grievances

A student who believes a grade has been recorded incorrectly should first raise the matter with the course instructor within five working days of the grade being published, since most discrepancies are simple data-entry corrections resolved at this stage. If unresolved, the student may escalate in writing to the department office, and from there, if still unresolved, to the Examinations Cell, which convenes a Grievance Redressal Committee for academic grade disputes. The Student Code of Conduct describes how grievance committees are constituted and the timelines they follow more generally.
