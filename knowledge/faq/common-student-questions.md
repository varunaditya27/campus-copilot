# Frequently Asked Questions

## How do I get a duplicate or replacement student ID card?

Report the lost card to the Student Services Desk in the Main Academic Block as soon as you notice it missing, so the card can be deactivated for library borrowing, hostel access, and exam-hall entry — the same card serves as both your student ID and your library card. A replacement is issued within three working days for a fee of ₹150, payable at the Student Services Desk or through the online fee portal. Bring a copy of your admission letter or another photo ID if your original card cannot be located.

## How do I request an official transcript?

Submit a transcript request through the Registrar's Office examinations cell, either in person or through the academic records section of the student portal. Standard processing takes five working days and costs ₹200 per copy; an expedited two-day turnaround is available for ₹400. Transcripts covering a semester whose results are still under revaluation cannot be issued until the revaluation process closes.

## How do I get a bonafide certificate?

Bonafide certificates (confirming your enrollment status, typically needed for bank loans, visa applications, or scholarship paperwork) are issued by the Registrar's Office on request, generally within two working days, at no charge for the first two requests each academic year and ₹100 per copy thereafter. Requests can be submitted at the Registrar's counter or through the student portal's certificate-request form; specify the exact purpose, as some external agencies require particular wording.

## What do I do if two of my classes are scheduled at the same time?

Report a timetable clash to your department office as soon as it appears, ideally within the first week of the semester. The department will either adjust your section assignment for one of the courses or, if the clash is between a core course and an elective, help you swap into an alternate elective slot during the add/drop window. Timetable clashes are far easier to resolve before the add/drop window closes than afterward.

## When are semester fees due, and what happens if I pay late?

Semester fees, which run to several tens of thousands of rupees depending on program and department, are due within the first two weeks of each semester, on dates published by the Registrar's Office ahead of registration. A late payment window of ten additional days is available with a late fee of ₹500 per week or part-week. Fees unpaid beyond that window can result in a hold on exam registration and access to the online portal; students facing genuine financial difficulty should contact the Dean of Student Affairs office before the deadline to discuss installment options or applicable financial-aid schemes, rather than after a hold has already been placed.

## What happens if I miss an internal assessment or quiz?

Contact your course instructor before the assessment if possible, or within 48 hours afterward if that isn't feasible, with supporting documentation (a medical certificate, for example). Instructors have discretion to offer a makeup assessment, prorate your internal marks based on remaining assessments, or apply the department's standard missed-assessment policy, whichever is most appropriate to the circumstances. An unexcused absence from an internal assessment is typically recorded as zero for that component.

## Can I change my hostel room?

Room-change requests go through your hostel's warden, using the room-change form available at the hostel office. Requests are reviewed at the end of each semester as part of the regular room-allocation cycle, when vacancies are most available; mid-semester changes are granted only for documented reasons such as a medical need, a serious roommate conflict that mediation hasn't resolved, or a maintenance issue that makes a room temporarily uninhabitable. Hostel blocks are Ashoka, Nilgiri, Kaveri, and Vindhya, and cross-block transfers are subject to space availability in the destination block.

## I lost something on campus — what do I do?

Check with the Student Services Desk first; items found in classrooms, the library, and common areas are typically turned in there and logged. For items lost in the library specifically, the circulation desk keeps its own found-items log. For anything lost in the Sports Complex or Student Activity Centre, check with the facility's front desk. Unclaimed items are held for one semester before being donated or disposed of.

## Can I appeal a grade I think is wrong?

Yes. First request a review from the course instructor, who can correct a clerical or calculation error directly. If the disagreement isn't resolved, you can file a formal revaluation request through the Registrar's examinations cell within seven calendar days of results being published. Revaluation involves a second evaluator reviewing your answer script; your grade can move up, down, or stay the same as a result, and the ₹300 revaluation fee is refunded only if your grade improves by a full letter grade or more. Non-academic grievances about how a course was conducted, as opposed to a specific grade, should go to the department office or the General Grievance Redressal Committee instead.

## What is the minimum attendance requirement, and what if I fall short?

Students are required to maintain at least 75% attendance in each course to be eligible to sit for that course's end-semester examination. Attendance shortfalls are flagged by the department partway through the semester so students have time to recover; a student who still falls below 75% by the end of the semester may be barred from the exam, subject to the department's condonation process for documented medical or other exceptional circumstances. Talk to your class advisor as soon as an attendance issue arises rather than waiting for the end of the semester.

## How do I find out my CGPA or check my grades?

Grades and cumulative GPA are published through the academic-records section of the student portal after each semester's results are finalized. The grading scale runs O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4), and F (0). If a grade appears missing or incorrect, contact the Registrar's examinations cell before assuming it's an error in your favor or otherwise.

## Who do I contact for a general problem that doesn't fit any of these categories?

The Student Services Desk in the Main Academic Block is the general first point of contact for most day-to-day questions and can direct you to the right office — the Registrar's Office, your department office, the Dean of Student Affairs, or a hostel warden — if it can't resolve the matter directly.
