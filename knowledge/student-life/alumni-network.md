# Alumni Network and Mentorship

## Purpose

The alumni network exists to keep graduates connected to the university and to each other, and to channel that connection back toward current students in the form of mentorship, career guidance, guest teaching, and financial giving. Every student who completes a degree at the university is automatically enrolled as a member of the Alumni Association upon graduation; there is no separate application.

## Alumni Association

The Alumni Association is the body responsible for maintaining contact with graduates, organizing alumni events, and coordinating giving-back programs. It is run by a small permanent staff based in the Student Activity Centre, supported by an elected Alumni Council of nine members who serve two-year terms and represent each of the five engineering departments plus a general seat.

The Association maintains a members' directory, sends a quarterly newsletter, and organizes the annual Alumni Meet held on the first Saturday of December, which typically draws several hundred returning graduates for a day of campus tours, department reunions, and an evening dinner on the Sports Complex grounds.

## Mentorship Program

Current students in their fifth semester or later can opt into the structured mentorship program, which pairs a student with an alumnus or alumna working in a related field. Enrollment opens twice a year, in the first two weeks of August and January, through an interest form available at the Student Services Desk and on the Association's noticeboard in the Student Activity Centre.

A typical mentorship relationship proceeds as follows:

1. The student submits an interest form indicating department, area of interest, and what they hope to get out of the relationship — technical guidance, career advice, or general professional mentorship.
2. The Alumni Association's mentorship coordinator matches students with mentors based on department, stated interest area, and career goals, typically within three to four weeks of the enrollment window closing.
3. Student and mentor have an introductory conversation, usually a video call, to set expectations.
4. The pair maintains periodic check-ins, commonly monthly, over the course of a semester or academic year.
5. At the end of the term, the relationship can be extended, formally closed, or allowed to continue informally.

Because matching depends on which alumni have volunteered as mentors in a given cycle, not every interest area can be guaranteed a match; students who are not matched in one cycle are given priority consideration in the next.

## Guest Lectures and Industry Talks

Alumni are regularly invited back to campus to give guest lectures, sit on panel discussions during the annual technical fest, and contribute to career-development sessions organized jointly with the Placement Cell. Departments maintain their own lists of alumni willing to speak, and a faculty coordinator in each department is responsible for scheduling at least two alumni-led sessions per semester.

## Giving Back

Alumni contribute to the institution in several ways beyond mentorship and guest teaching. A number of scholarships described in the university's financial support programs are funded in part or in full by alumni donors or alumni-founded companies. Alumni have also funded equipment upgrades and named research spaces in several of the university's laboratories and research centres, and many companies founded or led by alumni recruit actively through the Placement Cell each hiring season.

Donations to the Alumni Association's giving fund can be made online through the Association's portal or by contacting the Association office directly; donors may direct a gift toward a specific scholarship, department, or general fund.

## Staying Connected After Graduation

In the final semester, graduating students are enrolled in the Alumni Association's communication channels and given access to the alumni directory, subject to opting in during the exit formalities processed by the Registrar's Office. From that point, the Association becomes the primary point of contact for reunions, newsletters, mentorship opportunities as a mentor rather than a mentee, and giving programs. Students with questions about the transition from current-student services to alumni services should contact the Alumni Association office directly.
