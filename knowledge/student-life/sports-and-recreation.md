# Sports and Recreation

## Overview

The Sports Complex is the hub for both competitive and recreational athletics on campus, run by the Sports Office under the Dean of Student Affairs. Alongside the complex itself, the university fields inter-collegiate teams in six sports, runs continuous intramural leagues, and hosts the annual Sports Fest that closes out each academic year.

## Facilities

The Sports Complex includes an outdoor multi-purpose ground used for football, cricket, and athletics practice; four indoor courts split between badminton, basketball, and table tennis; a fitness centre with strength and cardio equipment maintained by a rotating staff of two trainers; and a swimming pool that operates seasonally, generally from February through November, closing over the winter months for maintenance.

Facility booking for team practice and club-organized sessions is coordinated through the Sports Office booking desk, located at the complex's main entrance, and slots can be reserved up to a week in advance. Casual and recreational use during posted open hours generally does not require advance booking, subject to available capacity — the fitness centre and indoor courts in particular can fill up during peak evening hours between 5:00 and 8:00 p.m.

## Institutional Teams

The university fields inter-collegiate teams in cricket, football, basketball, badminton, athletics, and chess. Trials are held during the first two weeks of the odd semester each year, conducted by the Sports Office in coordination with the coaching staff for each sport; results are posted on the Sports Office noticeboard within a week of trials concluding. Selected students commit to a regular practice schedule — typically three to four sessions a week during active season — and represent the university at inter-collegiate tournaments organized by the regional collegiate sports association.

## Intramural Leagues

Alongside the six institutional teams, department-level intramural leagues run through the academic year in football, cricket, basketball, badminton, and table tennis, culminating each year in the Sports Fest. Intramurals are open to any interested student regardless of institutional-team status and are organized on a rotating basis by student sports representatives from each department, with matches generally scheduled on weekend afternoons to avoid conflicts with coursework.

## Recreational Use

Outside of scheduled practice and tournament bookings, the Sports Complex is open for casual student use during posted hours, typically 6:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. on weekdays and 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. on weekends. Students should follow facility-specific safety rules — the swimming pool operates only during posted lifeguard hours and unsupervised use is not permitted, and first-time gym users are required to complete a brief equipment orientation with Sports Office staff before using the fitness centre unsupervised.

## Balancing Sports and Academics

Students on institutional teams, particularly those with travel commitments for inter-collegiate tournaments, are expected to coordinate attendance accommodations with their course instructors well in advance of any travel. The Sports Office issues an official participation letter for tournament travel that students can present to instructors and to the Office of the Registrar; attendance shortfalls arising from official sports commitments are generally handled the same way as other university-sanctioned absences, though final discretion rests with the instructor and department. Students juggling regular practice alongside a full course load are encouraged to plan their semester workload with their faculty advisor at the start of each term, particularly during the run-up to inter-collegiate tournaments or the Sports Fest.
