# Anti-Ragging and Grievance Redressal

## Anti-Ragging Policy

Ragging — any act that causes physical or psychological harm, humiliation, or harassment of a student, particularly one that targets new or junior students — is strictly prohibited anywhere on campus, in hostels, or in any university-affiliated setting, in any form. This includes verbal abuse, physical intimidation, forced acts, online harassment through social media or messaging groups, and any form of ragging conducted within hostel blocks (Ashoka, Nilgiri, Kaveri, or Vindhya) or common areas. The policy applies equally to senior students, student clubs, and informal groups; ragging conducted "in fun" or as an unofficial welcome tradition is not exempt.

Every incoming batch is briefed on this policy during orientation week, and anti-ragging notices are posted prominently in every hostel block, the Main Academic Block, and departmental corridors. All students, including seniors, sign an anti-ragging affidavit at the start of each academic year.

### Reporting Ragging

A student experiencing or witnessing ragging should report it immediately through any of the following channels:

- the campus Anti-Ragging Committee, whose contact numbers and email are published during orientation and posted in every hostel block and on departmental noticeboards;
- the hostel warden, for incidents occurring within a hostel block;
- the Dean of Student Affairs office directly, in person or by phone;
- campus security's 24-hour emergency line, for any incident involving an immediate safety concern.

Reports can be made confidentially, including anonymously through a drop-box maintained at the Student Activity Centre and an anonymous reporting form linked from the Dean of Student Affairs' office page. The university's anti-ragging process is designed to protect the identity of the reporting student wherever possible, and retaliation of any kind against a student who reports ragging in good faith — whether by the accused, their associates, or anyone else — is itself treated as a severe violation of the Student Code of Conduct, subject to immediate disciplinary action up to expulsion.

A report does not need to include every detail up front. The Anti-Ragging Committee's role is to take the report seriously, ensure the reporting student's immediate safety, and carry out any necessary investigation; a student reporting an incident is never expected to first establish or argue that what happened "counts" as ragging before receiving help.

### Anti-Ragging Committee Process

```text
Incident reported (in person, by phone, or anonymously)
      ↓
Immediate safety check and, if needed, separation of the parties involved
      ↓
Formal inquiry by the Anti-Ragging Committee
      ↓
Findings and recommended action, forwarded to the Dean of Student Affairs
      ↓
Disciplinary action, ranging from a warning to suspension or expulsion,
and referral to local authorities where the law requires it
```

The Anti-Ragging Committee aims to complete its initial safety assessment within 24 hours of a report and to conclude a formal inquiry within two weeks, though complex cases may take longer. Interim measures — such as a hostel room change for the affected student, or restricting the accused student's access to certain facilities pending inquiry — can be put in place immediately, without waiting for the inquiry to conclude.

## Internal Complaints Committee (Sexual Harassment)

The Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) handles complaints of sexual harassment involving students, faculty, or staff, in accordance with the university's documented sexual-harassment policy. The ICC is chaired by a senior faculty member and includes faculty, staff, and student representation, along with an external member not affiliated with the university, as required by regulation.

Complaints may be submitted in writing to the ICC directly, through the Dean of Student Affairs office, or through the same anonymous reporting form used for ragging complaints. The ICC follows a confidential process for reporting, investigation, and resolution, and aims to complete an inquiry within 90 days of a complaint being filed. As with ragging, retaliation against a student who files a complaint in good faith is itself a serious disciplinary offense.

## General Grievance Redressal Committee

For grievances that are not safety-critical — academic disputes, fee disagreements, hostel administrative issues, or other non-emergency concerns — the university maintains a General Grievance Redressal Committee, distinct from the Anti-Ragging Committee and the ICC. A typical process:

```text
Submit grievance in writing through the Student Services Desk
or the grievance portal
      ↓
Acknowledgment within three working days
      ↓
Review and investigation by the relevant office
      ↓
Resolution or escalation to the Dean of Student Affairs
      ↓
Written communication of the outcome to the student,
generally within four weeks of submission
```

Grade-specific academic grievances follow the grade-review and revaluation process administered by the Registrar's examinations cell rather than this general committee. The General Grievance Redressal Committee handles broader administrative and interpersonal grievances that do not fall under a more specific process.

## A Note on Priority

Any concern that could plausibly involve ragging, harassment, or an immediate threat to a student's safety takes precedence over all other university processes, including routine academic or administrative matters that might otherwise be in progress at the same time. Students, staff, and faculty are all expected to treat such a report as urgent and to route it to real help — the Anti-Ragging Committee, the ICC, the hostel warden, the Dean of Student Affairs, or campus security — without delay.
