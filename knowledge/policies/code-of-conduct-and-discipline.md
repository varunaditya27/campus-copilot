# Student Code of Conduct and Discipline

> **Demo corpus document:** This is a fictionalized educational policy for the Campus Copilot workshop, not a real disciplinary regulation.

## Purpose

The Student Code of Conduct sets baseline expectations for behavior across academic, hostel, and campus-wide settings, so that the community can function safely and fairly. It complements [`digital-conduct-and-computing.md`](digital-conduct-and-computing.md), which covers computing-specific conduct, and [`anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md`](anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md), which covers safety-critical reporting.

## General Expectations

Students are expected to:

- treat faculty, staff, fellow students, and campus visitors with respect;
- follow academic-integrity requirements in coursework and examinations (see [`examinations-and-evaluation.md`](../academics/examinations-and-evaluation.md) and [`digital-conduct-and-computing.md`](digital-conduct-and-computing.md));
- comply with hostel, laboratory, and facility-specific rules;
- use campus resources responsibly;
- report safety concerns rather than handling them informally when the situation warrants escalation.

## Categories of Misconduct

The demonstration policy groups misconduct into broad categories, each handled with escalating seriousness:

- **Minor conduct issues** — for example, repeated lateness disrupting a class, minor property carelessness — usually handled informally by the instructor, warden, or relevant staff member.
- **Moderate violations** — for example, unauthorized access to a restricted facility, repeated disregard for posted rules — typically escalated to the department office or Dean of Student Affairs.
- **Serious violations** — for example, academic dishonesty, harassment, property damage, safety violations — handled by a formal Disciplinary Committee process.
- **Severe violations** — for example, violence, ragging, or activity that endangers others — handled with the highest priority, often involving the Dean of Student Affairs, security, and, where applicable, external authorities.

## Disciplinary Committee Process

A formal disciplinary matter generally follows:

```text
Incident reported or observed
      ↓
Preliminary review
      ↓
Notice to the student(s) involved
      ↓
Hearing before the Disciplinary Committee
      ↓
Decision
      ↓
Right to appeal (where applicable)
```

Outcomes can range from a warning to suspension or, in severe cases, expulsion, depending on the severity and the institution's documented process. Campus Copilot should never predict or imply a specific outcome for an actual reported incident — that is exclusively the Disciplinary Committee's role.

## Reporting a Concern

A student who witnesses or experiences misconduct should report it through the appropriate channel:

- academic-integrity concerns → the course instructor or department office;
- hostel-related concerns → the block warden;
- safety, harassment, or ragging concerns → see [`anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md`](anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md) for the dedicated, higher-priority process that applies to these categories specifically.

## What Campus Copilot Should Do When a Student Describes a Serious Incident

If a student describes something that sounds like harassment, ragging, or a safety threat, Campus Copilot should prioritize directing them to the correct reporting channel immediately, rather than treating it as a routine knowledge-retrieval question. See [`anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md`](anti-ragging-and-grievance-redressal.md) for the specific escalation path.
