# Student Code of Conduct and Discipline

## Purpose

The Student Code of Conduct sets baseline expectations for behavior across academic, hostel, and campus-wide settings, so that the community can function safely and fairly. It works alongside the Digital Conduct and Responsible Computing policy, which covers computing-specific conduct, and the Anti-Ragging and Grievance Redressal policy, which covers safety-critical reporting for ragging and harassment. Together these documents form the basis of the university's disciplinary framework, administered under the authority of the Dean of Student Affairs.

## General Expectations

Students are expected to:

- treat faculty, staff, fellow students, and campus visitors with courtesy and respect;
- follow academic-integrity requirements in coursework and examinations, including the university's examination regulations and the rules governing use of digital tools;
- comply with hostel, laboratory, and facility-specific rules, including those posted by block wardens and laboratory in-charges;
- use campus resources — library material, laboratory equipment, sports facilities, and common spaces — responsibly and considerately;
- report safety concerns to the appropriate office rather than attempting to resolve them informally when the situation calls for escalation.

## Categories of Misconduct

Misconduct is grouped into four broad categories, each handled with escalating seriousness and by a different level of authority.

### Minor Conduct Issues

Examples include repeated lateness disrupting a class, minor carelessness with shared property, or failure to follow a posted facility rule after a first reminder. These are usually handled informally by the instructor, hostel warden, or relevant staff member, typically through a verbal or written warning recorded in the department or hostel office.

### Moderate Violations

Examples include unauthorized access to a restricted facility (a laboratory outside permitted hours, a staff-only area), repeated disregard for posted rules after a formal warning, or minor damage to university property. These are typically escalated to the department office or the Dean of Student Affairs, and may result in a fine, restitution for damaged property, or a formal written warning placed on the student's disciplinary record.

### Serious Violations

Examples include academic dishonesty (plagiarism, use of unauthorized material in an examination, impersonation in an assessment), harassment, significant property damage, or safety violations in a laboratory or workshop. These are handled through a formal hearing before the Disciplinary Committee, a standing body convened by the Dean of Student Affairs and comprising a chairperson, two faculty members drawn from outside the student's own department, and a representative of the Registrar's Office.

### Severe Violations

Examples include violence, ragging in any form, or activity that endangers the safety of others. These are treated with the highest priority and are handled jointly by the Dean of Student Affairs, campus security, and, where the law requires it, external authorities. A severe violation can result in suspension or expulsion regardless of whether it is a student's first recorded disciplinary matter.

## Disciplinary Committee Process

A formal disciplinary matter generally proceeds through the following stages:

```text
Incident reported or observed
      ↓
Preliminary review by the department or Dean of Student Affairs
      ↓
Written notice to the student(s) involved, stating the allegation
      ↓
Hearing before the Disciplinary Committee, where the student may present their account
      ↓
Decision, communicated in writing within ten working days of the hearing
      ↓
Right to appeal to the Dean of Academics within seven working days of the decision
```

Outcomes range from a formal warning, to a fine or restitution, to suspension for a defined period, to expulsion in the most severe or repeated cases. The specific outcome for any matter depends on its facts and is determined solely by the Disciplinary Committee following its hearing; no informal indication of a likely outcome is given before that process concludes.

## Reporting a Concern

A student who witnesses or experiences misconduct should report it through the appropriate channel:

- academic-integrity concerns should go to the course instructor or the department office;
- hostel-related concerns should go to the block warden;
- safety, harassment, or ragging concerns require immediate escalation through the dedicated reporting channels described below, rather than the general process used for other categories of misconduct.

## Ragging, Harassment, and Safety Concerns

Ragging, harassment, and any concern involving a student's immediate physical or psychological safety are treated as a distinct, higher-priority category, separate from routine disciplinary matters. Reports in this category are handled by the Anti-Ragging Committee, the Internal Complaints Committee, or the Dean of Student Affairs office directly, depending on the nature of the concern, and can be made confidentially. Any student who witnesses or experiences this kind of incident should report it immediately rather than waiting to see whether it recurs; the full reporting channels, confidentiality protections, and the university's zero-tolerance stance on retaliation against a reporting student are set out in the Anti-Ragging and Grievance Redressal policy.
